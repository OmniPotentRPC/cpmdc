=================================
Relax a cell at a constant cutoff
=================================



Goal
----

Give an ASE cell filter, a LAMMPS ``fix box/relax`` or ``fix npt``, or GROMACS
pressure coupling a stress that follows the energy of a periodic OpenCPMD deck.
Turn on the constant-cutoff kinetic functional of `Bernasconi et al., J. Phys.
Chem. Solids 56, 501 (1995) <https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-3697(94)00228-2>`_,
which OpenCPMD reads as ``CONSTANT CUTOFF`` in ``&SYSTEM``.
It is off by default, and a message without it renders the same deck as before.

Why a fixed cutoff misleads a cell optimizer
--------------------------------------------

OpenCPMD's stress is the derivative of the energy at a fixed set of plane waves.
A new cell is a new setup of the deck at the same cutoff, which holds a
different set, so the energy of the next cell is not the energy the stress
predicted.
The difference is the Pulay stress.
On the 40 Ry periodic water deck it is about 16 eV divided by the volume, 1.6e-2
eV/Angstrom\ :sup:`3`  in the 10 Angstrom cube, and it grows as the cell shrinks: a cell
filter shrinks the cell while the energy rises.

The functional replaces G\ :sup:`2`  in the kinetic energy by

::

    G^2 + A [1 + erf((G^2 - E0) / sigma)]

with A, sigma, and E0 in Ry.
A plane wave above E0 costs up to 2A more, so the orbitals hold almost no weight
above E0 in any cell, and the energy belongs to the constant cutoff E0 while the
basis keeps the cutoff of the deck.
``kin_energy``, ``vpsi``, and the kinetic stress of OpenCPMD apply the term and its
derivative, so the stress is the derivative of that energy.
The OpenCPMD manual prints the argument of erf as ``G^2 / 2 - E0 / sigma``; the
code in ``kin_energy_utils.mod.F90`` uses ``(G^2 - E0) / sigma``.

From ASE or Python
------------------

Pass E0 in Ry:

.. code:: python

    from ase import Atoms
    from ase.filters import FrechetCellFilter
    from ase.optimize import BFGS
    from cpmdc.ase import CPMDCCalculator

    water = [[0.0, 0.0, 0.1173], [0.0, 0.7572, -0.4692], [0.0, -0.7572, -0.4692]]
    atoms = Atoms("OHH", positions=water, cell=[10.0, 10.0, 10.0], pbc=True)
    atoms.rattle(0.05, seed=17)
    with CPMDCCalculator("water.periodic.bin", constant_cutoff=30.0) as calc:
        atoms.calc = calc
        with BFGS(FrechetCellFilter(atoms), logfile=None) as opt:
            opt.run(fmax=0.01, steps=80)
        print(opt.nsteps, atoms.get_volume())

The keyword also takes a mapping or a ``cpmdc.ConstantCutoff`` that sets A or
sigma.
For example, ``ConstantCutoff(30.0, height_ry=2000.0, width_ry=3.0)`` sets both.
``UnitCellFilter`` and ``ase.md.npt.NPT`` read the same stress.
``Session`` and ``Potential`` take the same keyword.
``calc.set(constant_cutoff=...)`` opens a new session; ``None`` turns the functional
off.

From a message: LAMMPS, GROMACS, i-PI, and C
--------------------------------------------

Hosts that read a ``CPMDParams`` file take the functional from the ``system``
section of the message, with A, sigma, and E0 in that order:

.. code:: text

    ( system = ( symmetry = 1, angstrom = true, cutOffRy = 40.0,
        cell = [10.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0],
        constantCutoff = [2000.0, 3.0, 30.0] ) ),

``pair_style cpmdc``, ``cpmdc.lammps.FixExternal``, ``libcpmdc_gromacs``, and the
socket drivers then evaluate it.
A C host can set it on a session before its first step instead:

.. code:: c

    CPMDCSession *s = cpmdc_session_create(params_bytes, params_size);
    if (!s || cpmdc_session_set_constant_cutoff(s, 30.0, 0.0, 0.0) != 0)
      fprintf(stderr, "%s\n", cpmdc_last_error());

A height or width of 0 selects the defaults below.
A job TOML for ``tools/job_toml.py`` takes a ``[constant_cutoff]`` table with
``cutoff_ry``, and optionally ``height_ry`` and ``width_ry``.

Choose E0, A, and sigma
-----------------------

- E0 + 2 sigma must not exceed the cutoff of the deck; ``cpmdc`` refuses the
  setting otherwise, with the three numbers in the message.
  Keep the deck cutoff a little above E0, for example E0 = 30 Ry on a 40 Ry
  deck.

- Without A, ``cpmdc`` uses 50 times the deck cutoff; without sigma, E0 / 10.
  The penalty then rises from near 0 at E0 - 2 sigma to 2A at E0 + 2 sigma.

- A larger A brings the stress closer to dE/dV, but a very large A reaches below
  E0 through the tail of erf and moves the energy itself.

- Converge the physics in E0, not in the deck cutoff.
  The energy is that of a basis a little below E0, so it lies above the energy
  of the plain deck: -443.69 eV for the water at E0 = 30 Ry on the 40 Ry deck,
  against -459.24 eV for the plain 40 Ry deck.
  Compare energies only at the same E0, A, and sigma.

- A message that gives ``&SYSTEM`` as raw text, or that already sets
  ``system.constantCutoff``, cannot take ``constant_cutoff=`` as well.

Without ``tools/opencpmd_constant_cutoff_precon.patch`` the ODIIS preconditioner
ignores the penalty, ODIIS stops at its step limit, and ``cpmdc`` continues with
PCG, which is slower; the :doc:`archive how-to <opencpmd-archive>` lists
the patch.

--------------------------

cutoffs, as written and with E0 = 0.75 times the cutoff and the default A and
sigma, on an Intel Xeon virtual machine with 8 vCPUs.
The full output is in ``bench/results/constant_cutoff_water.org``.

At a fixed cutoff the stress exceeds the slope of the energy by 2.25e-2
eV/Angstrom\ :sup:`3`  at 25 Ry and still by 1.84e-3 eV/Angstrom\ :sup:`3`  at 80 Ry.
At a constant cutoff it is below 2.2e-4 eV/Angstrom\ :sup:`3`  and follows the sign of
the slope; the stress that remains is the internal stress of the unrelaxed
bonds.
A stress call costs 1.0 to 1.33 times as much as at the fixed cutoff.

At every fixed cutoff the cell collapses within 12 steps, and from 40 Ry up the
energy ends higher than it started.
At a constant cutoff every run converges between 919 and 998 Angstrom\ :sup:`3`\: one
water in a 10 Angstrom box has almost no intermolecular stress.
At 40 Ry, BFGS overshoots on that flat volume mode, so 20 of its 53 steps raise
the energy, by at most 0.041 eV; the first six steps lower it every time.

density with a published PBE value.
