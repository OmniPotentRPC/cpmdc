=========================
Atoms in an isolated cell
=========================


An isolated deck (``SYMMETRY 0``, a Poisson solver, ``CLUSTER``, ``SURFACE`` or
``POLYMER``) holds the density on a box of fixed size with its origin at a corner,
so a host whose atoms drift towards a face, or leave the box, puts density
across that face.
The energy then depends on where the molecule sits in the box, not only on its
shape, and an MD host sees a force that pushes the molecule about.

What recentring computes
------------------------

With ``CPMDC_RECENTER`` at ``mass`` or ``geometry`` the energy is that of the atoms
moved to the middle of the box, a function of their relative positions only, and
the forces are its gradient with respect to the caller's coordinates: the net
force the grid leaves is projected out, the fraction of it each atom carries
given by its weight in the centre.
Energy, forces and ``sum_i x_i (x) f_i`` then do not change when the caller
translates every atom.

What drift costs without it
---------------------------

On one BLYP water in the 10 Angstrom Hockney box with ``off``, a hydrogen 0.5
Angstrom from a face raises the energy by 5.0e-4 Hartree, a hydrogen 0.5
Angstrom past it lowers it by 8.4e-3 Hartree with a net force of 0.17
Hartree/Bohr, and the water about the corner lowers it by 7.9e-2 Hartree; with ``mass`` all of these give the energy of the centred molecule to 1.1e-14 Hartree (``bench/results/cluster_drift_scan.org``).
:doc:`cpmdc inside MD engines <md-engines>` shows the same effect in
GROMACS runs before and after recentring.

:doc:`The environment reference <../reference/environment>` lists the
values of ``CPMDC_RECENTER`` and ``CPMDC_RECENTER_MARGIN`` and the rules they
follow.
