=========================
CSVR thermostat reference
=========================


The ionic CSVR thermostat is declared in
``include/cpmdc_csvr.h`` and implemented in ``src/cpmdc_csvr.c``.
It is a separate library, ``libcpmdc_csvr``, and it is not part of ``libcpmdc``.
A session never calls it.
Leaving it out of a calculation leaves energies and forces unchanged.
The update is the canonical velocity rescaling of Bussi, Donadio, and
Parrinello, J. Chem. Phys. 126, 014101 (2007), `arXiv:0803.4060 <https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4060>`_.
The finite-time propagator for ``y(t)`` is equation (21) in the arXiv HTML and
equation (A6) in the arXiv PDF and the journal.
The rescaling factor ``alpha^2`` is equation (22) in the arXiv HTML and equation
(A7) in the arXiv PDF and the journal.

Update
------

The host supplies the kinetic energy of the degrees it wants to thermostat.
``temperature`` is Boltzmann's constant times the target temperature, in that same
energy unit.
``time_step`` and ``time_constant`` share one time unit.
The propagation factor is ``c = exp(-time_step / time_constant)``.
A positive infinite time constant, or a zero time step, sets ``c`` to 1.
The target kinetic energy is ``ndof * temperature / 2``.
With ``s = (1 - c) * temperature / (2 * kinetic)``, one standard normal ``R1``, and
``S`` the sum of squares of the other ``ndof - 1`` standard normals,

::

    scale^2 = (sqrt(c) + R1 * sqrt(s))^2 + s * S
    work = kinetic * (1 - scale^2)

``S`` is drawn as twice a unit-scale gamma sample of shape ``(ndof - 1) / 2``.
That factor of two is the scale of a chi-squared draw.
The paragraph after the rescaling factor says the other ``N_f - 1`` squares can be
drawn from the gamma density printed there.
That density is the unit-scale density, whose mean is the shape, so the sum of
squares is twice that draw.
ASE does the same with ``2 * standard_gamma``.
One degree of freedom has no remaining squares, so ``S`` is 0 and no gamma sample
is drawn.
The first normal is ``R1``, and the gamma draw, when there is one, comes after it.
A zero temperature or ``c`` of 1 makes the noise terms zero.
The scale is then ``sqrt(c)``, the work is ``kinetic * (1 - c)``, and the generator
is not advanced.
The returned scale is the positive square root.
The host multiplies every thermostatted velocity component by that scale.
``work`` is the energy removed from those degrees.
The effective energy is the Hamiltonian plus the sum of ``work`` along the
trajectory.
Section II C of the 2007 paper uses that sum to see integrator error.
The rescaling itself does not change it.

Calls
-----

.. table::

    +----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
    | Call                                                                                         | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
    +==============================================================================================+====================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================+
    | ``cpmdc_csvr_rng_seed(rng, seed)``                                                           | Fill a caller-owned generator. Any 64-bit seed is accepted, including zero. Returns 0, or -1 when ``rng`` is NULL, and a failure writes nothing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
    +----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
    | ``cpmdc_csvr_scale(kinetic, ndof, temperature, time_step, time_constant, rng, scale, work)`` | Write the positive scale and the thermostat work. Returns 0, or -1 when a pointer is NULL, ``rng`` was not seeded, ``ndof`` is below 1, ``kinetic`` is not finite and positive, ``temperature`` is not finite and non-negative, ``time_step`` is not finite and non-negative, ``time_constant`` is not positive, or a result is not finite. A failure writes nothing and does not advance ``rng``. |
    +----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

Both functions have C linkage.
The header compiles as C and as C++.
Neither function writes ``cpmdc_last_error()``.
The return code is the whole report.
Link ``libcpmdc_csvr`` and ``libm``.
The installed header is ``include/cpmdc/cpmdc_csvr.h``.

Generator
---------

The generator is a plain object the host allocates.
Copying the bytes replays the same later draws.
Do not share one object across threads.
A scale call reads and writes only that object.

Fields of ``cpmdc_csvr_rng``.

.. table::

    +------------------+-------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
    | Field            | Type                    | Meaning                                                                        |
    +==================+=========================+================================================================================+
    | ``state``        | four ``uint64_t`` words | Generator state. All zeros means the object has not been seeded.               |
    +------------------+-------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
    | ``spare_normal`` | ``double``              | The unused value of a Box-Muller pair, in the same units as a standard normal. |
    +------------------+-------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
    | ``spare_ready``  | ``int``                 | One when ``spare_normal`` has not been consumed, otherwise zero.               |
    +------------------+-------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

Python
------

``cpmdc.csvr.Rng`` and ``cpmdc.csvr.scale`` call this library.
``temperature_K`` and ``taut`` are the ASE keyword names.
``timestep`` uses the same time unit as ``taut``.
``kB`` converts one kelvin into the energy unit of the kinetic energy.
``cpmdc.csvr.HARTREE_PER_KELVIN`` is that conversion for Hartree.
ASE stores ``(scale^2 - 1) * kinetic`` in ``transferred_energy``.
``work`` is the opposite sign, so a host that wants the ASE accumulator subtracts
``work``.
The binding raises ``TypeError``, ``ValueError``, or ``CPMDCError`` and does not
advance the generator when the C call returns -1.
The loader tries four places, in order.
``CPMDC_CSVR_LIBRARY`` is used when it names a file.
Next is the copy shipped next to the package, under ``.cpmdc.mesonpy.libs`` beside
the package directory.
Next, ``ctypes.util.find_library`` is given ``libcpmdc_csvr`` without the ``lib``
prefix, and the name it returns is passed to ``ctypes.CDLL`` even when that name
is not a file.
Last, ``build/`` is a development fallback, in the working directory and at the
repository root.
A library that loads but lacks ``cpmdc_csvr_rng_seed`` or ``cpmdc_csvr_scale``
raises ``CPMDCError``.

Other codes
-----------

CP2K selects this thermostat with ``MOTION/MD/THERMOSTAT TYPE CSVR``.
The default type on that keyword is ``NOSE``.
``CSVR/TIMECON`` is the time constant in femtoseconds.
Its default is 1000 fs.
``csvr_init`` reads that value and divides it by half the integration time step,
because the thermostat is applied on both velocity-Verlet half steps.
``do_csvr`` passes ``taut = tau_csvr / dt_fact`` to ``rescaling_factor``.
``dt_fact`` starts at 1.
``rescaling_factor`` takes ``taut`` in units of how often it is called, so a host
that calls that routine once per step passes the time constant divided by the
time step.
The CP2K routine ``rescaling_factor`` in ``csvr_system_utils`` follows equation
(A7).
It sets the propagation factor to 0 when ``taut`` is 0.1 or smaller.
This library does not.
It keeps ``exp(-time_step / time_constant)`` for every positive finite constant.
CP2K also flips the sign of the scale for a single degree of freedom when the
normal draw is negative enough.
This library returns the positive root, as the paper's square root and ASE do.
CP2K forms the sum of squares by repeating a normal draw.
This library draws that sum from the gamma distribution.
CP2K's comment on the effective energy accumulates minus the kinetic-energy
increments, which is the sum of ``work``.
When the thermostat covers a system whose center of mass is fixed, CP2K tells
the caller to omit those degrees from the count and to keep the center-of-mass
momentum at zero.
Each holonomic constraint also comes off that count.
``compute_nfree`` subtracts intramolecular constraints and intermolecular
constraints that are not restraints, then the rotational and translational
degrees.
This library does not adjust the count.
The host passes ``ndof`` and applies the scale to those velocities.

The CPMD 4.3.0 manual thermostats ions with ``NOSE``, ``BERENDSEN``, ``LANGEVIN``, and
``TEMPCONTROL``.
It has no CSVR keyword.
This library does not render a deck line and does not add a feature id.
The host applies the scale between force evaluations.
The session path is unchanged.
