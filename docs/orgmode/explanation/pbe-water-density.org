#+TITLE: The PBE density of water against Miceli et al.
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil todo:nil num:nil author:nil title:t

[[file:constant-cutoff-npt.org][Density of water at a constant cutoff]] gives 826.1 +- 5.7 kg/m^3 for 32 PBE waters at 350 K and 1 bar from the 0.65 ps that ~cc100~ averages, and its standard error still grew with the block length.
Miceli, de Gironcoli, and Pasquarello found about 870 kg/m^3 at 349 K (reference 1).
This page makes a step cheaper, runs the 32 waters for longer, and measures what the statistics, the basis, and the pseudopotentials contribute to the 44 kg/m^3 between the two.
It does not measure the thermostat, the barostat, or the number of waters.
Every number comes from a record in ~bench/results~ that the text names, from the paper, or from arithmetic on those.

* What Miceli et al. ran
Miceli et al. ran Born-Oppenheimer dynamics of 64 waters in Quantum ESPRESSO with PBE, orbitals expanded to 85 Ry, and norm-conserving pseudopotentials for which they cite Troullier and Martins (reference 2); the paper does not name the files.
The nuclei are classical and move by the Beeman algorithm in 0.48 fs steps, and a Parrinello-Rahman barostat holds a cubic cell at zero pressure.
Each run starts from a configuration of classical NVT dynamics at 1 g/cm^3 and 300 K and equilibrates for about 5 ps at 350 K under a velocity rescaling that acts only when the cumulative mean temperature leaves 350 +- 20 K.
During the production runs, of 25 to 35 ps, the rescaling never acts, so they sample NpH.
The basis keeps a constant number of plane waves: the cutoff converges the stress within 1 kbar in a cell at 1 g/cm^3, and a run whose density fell during the equilibration started again from a larger cell "to restore the originally set higher cut-off".

Sec. V A gives the density: "the PBE functional gives an average density for liquid water of ∼0.87 g/cm^3 underestimating the experimental value by about 13%".
Fig. 4 plots it beside the densities of rVV10, and Sec. V C reads that figure as densities from 1.10 to 0.87 g/cm^3, the lower end for PBE.
No table of the paper lists the value, and the paper gives no error bar.
The caption of Fig. 5 gives the mean temperature of the PBE run, 349 K, and that of Fig. 4 puts every run within 345 +- 4 K.

~long32~ below differs from those runs in its length, its integrators (Langevin atoms and the MTK barostat at 350 K and 1 bar), its pseudopotentials (~O_MT_PBE~ and ~H_MT_PBE~ of the CPMD library), its basis (the constant cutoff E0 = 85 Ry on a 100 Ry deck), and its 32 waters.
The deuterium masses and the 1 fs steps do not enter, since the configurational averages of classical nuclei do not depend on the masses.

* Pseudopotentials
~O_MT_PBE~ and ~H_MT_PBE~ are Troullier-Martins pseudopotentials with core radii of 1.12 bohr for the s, p, and d channels of oxygen and 0.50 bohr for the s channel of hydrogen.
The decks of ~constant_cutoff_npt.py~ take the p channel of oxygen as local, with the s channel nonlocal, and the s channel of hydrogen as local.
~O.pbe-mt_fhi.UPF~ and ~H.pbe-mt_fhi.UPF~ of the Quantum ESPRESSO pseudopotential table are the FHI98PP Troullier-Martins pseudopotentials of the Abinit tables (reference 6), converted to UPF in 2012.
They take the d channel as local with projectors for s, p, and f, which vanish beyond 1.43 bohr for oxygen and 1.28 bohr for hydrogen.
CPMD reads both UPF files as they define their channels.

* The basis of Miceli et al.
With a height A of 1e-6 Ry the penalty of the constant cutoff vanishes, and with ~CPMDC_CELL_IN_PLACE~ above every strain of a run, each cell keeps the plane waves of the first setup: a basis of a constant number of plane waves, as in the runs of Miceli et al., whose effective cutoff falls as the cell grows.
~bench/results/fixed_basis_check.org~ checks that on one water in a 10 Angstrom cube at 40 Ry: at the setup cell the energy and pressure equal those of a plain 40 Ry setup within 2e-6 eV and 1e-4 kbar, and in the cell stretched by 5% in place the 14336 plane waves of the setup give the energy and pressure of a new setup at 40/1.05^2 = 36.28 Ry, which holds the same 14336, within 3e-6 eV and 2e-4 kbar.
No run here uses that basis.

Miceli et al. set their basis at 1 g/cm^3 and restarted a run whose density fell from a larger cell, so their production runs kept about the plane waves of 85 Ry at the density they sampled.
At 870 kg/m^3, a fixed 85 Ry setup of the FHI files gives a molecular pressure 1.15 kbar below the converged -1.62 kbar, and the plane waves of 85 Ry at 1000 kg/m^3 (77.46 Ry) give 2.15 kbar below it.
On the states of ~long32~, the fixed 85 Ry setup of the FHI files gives a molecular pressure 1.038 +- 0.021 kbar below that of E0 = 136 Ry, from 0.93 to 1.12 kbar below on the eight states.

* Where the gap goes
A setup whose molecular pressure lies dP above that of another on the same configurations holds the liquid less dense at 1 bar, by dP times the 48.6 +- 9.5 kg/m^3 per kbar of ~long32~.
On the states of ~long32~, taking the converged FHI files as the middle ground:

| Change, toward the runs of Miceli et al. | Molecular pressure, kbar | Density, kg/m^3 |
|------------------------------------------+--------------------------+-----------------|
| CPMD files at E0 = 85 Ry to FHI files at E0 = 136 Ry | -0.583 +- 0.253 | +28.3 +- 12.3 |
| of which CPMD files at E0 = 170 Ry to FHI files at E0 = 136 Ry | -0.482 +- 0.025 | +23.4 +- 1.2 |
| FHI files at E0 = 136 Ry to a fixed 85 Ry | -1.038 +- 0.021 | +50.4 +- 1.0 |
| Both | -1.621 | +78.7 |

The errors in the last column are those of the pressures; the 20% of the compressibility scales the column as a whole.
The measured changes of setup move the density by +78.7 kg/m^3, more than the 20.7 kg/m^3 between ~long32~ and Miceli et al.: 20.7 - 78.7 = -58.0 kg/m^3 is left, against a blocking error of at least 10.06 kg/m^3, 12.3 kg/m^3 from the scatter of the basis error of the CPMD files at E0 = 85 Ry, and 15.4 kg/m^3 from the compressibility, or 22.1 kg/m^3 together.
The largest change is the fixed basis, whose Pulay stress makes the liquid of Miceli et al. denser than a converged basis would, if their pseudopotentials are the FHI files; the paper does not name them.

Not measured here:
- the thermostat and the barostat, Langevin and MTK against Parrinello-Rahman in NpH;
- 32 waters against 64;
- 345 K against 349 K;
- the statistical error of Miceli et al., which the paper does not give;
- whether the linear response holds over the 1.6 kbar of the last row.

The two changes of setup that this page measures move the density by 28.3 and 50.4 kg/m^3, each more than the gap, and together they overshoot it by 58.0 kg/m^3, 2.6 times the 22.1 kg/m^3 of the combined uncertainty.
The linear response over the 1.6 kbar of the last row, a change of 9% in density at the compressibility of ~long32~, is the largest assumption of the column, and it is not measured here.
These numbers do not resolve a physical difference between the two PBE densities: the gap itself is 2.1 times a blocking error that is still a lower bound, since the blocking curve of ~long32~ has not levelled off.
